About this site

The purpose of this site is not to let AI decide what intervention should be used. It is designed to help practitioners structure real-world questions, connect them with existing evidence, and develop interventions that are both feasible and testable.

  • This site creates prompts for using AI to support Evidence-Based Policing / Evidence-Based Practice (EBP) design.
  • By entering the problem and what is currently known, you can generate a prompt that asks your AI to review relevant EBP resources and academic evidence, compare previous interventions, and organise possible intervention options.
  • The intervention options proposed by AI should primarily draw on approaches that have already been examined in research or practice. The tool is not mainly intended to ask AI to invent entirely new interventions from scratch.
  • If you already have a new intervention idea, the prompt can help compare it with related interventions, relevant criminological or behavioural theory, and prior evaluation methods, and can help turn the idea into a testable hypothesis.

Read this before entering any information

Do not enter personal data, identifiable case details, active investigation information, sensitive operational methods, unpublished internal information, or confidential material.

Where necessary, anonymise or generalise details using labels such as “Area A”, “Facility A”, or “Target Group A”. This page does not send your entries to an external server; the prompt is generated locally in your browser.

What to do after copying the prompt

  1. Select “Copy the generated prompt”.
  2. Open the AI service you normally use, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Copilot.
  3. Paste the prompt into a new chat and send it.
  4. Answer the AI’s follow-up questions and work through intervention options, measurement, and evaluation design.

This webpage does not itself send your information to an AI service. Use only AI services permitted by your organisation and follow all applicable information-security rules.
