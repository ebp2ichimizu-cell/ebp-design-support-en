EBP Design Support

Enter what you know. The site generates a structured prompt that you can paste into your usual AI to work through problem analysis, intervention options, measurement, and evaluation design.

What this tool does

This website does not itself conduct AI searches, analyse police data, or make policing decisions.

  • It is a prompt-building tool designed to help practitioners create a structured prompt that can be copied into an AI service that they are authorised by their organisation to use.
  • The generated prompt guides the AI through a staged Evidence-Based Policing process, including problem structuring, evidence searching, comparison of intervention options, outcome and measurement planning, and evaluation design.
  • The purpose is to support practitioner judgement — not to replace it.
Before using the generated prompt

Please use only AI services that are permitted under your organisation’s policies and approval requirements.

Do not enter or upload:

  • personal or identifiable information;
  • active investigation information;
  • sensitive operational or investigative methods;
  • unpublished or non-public police information;
  • confidential material; or
  • any other information that your organisation does not permit to be shared with an external AI service.

If such information is relevant to the problem, anonymise, generalise, or remove it before using the generated prompt.

Organisational information-governance, security, legal, ethical, and research requirements always take priority over this tool.

Copyright and use of external sources

The generated prompt is designed to use external EBP websites, academic publications, toolkits and guidance primarily for evidence searching, verification, summarisation and linking to original sources.

It instructs the AI not to reproduce, translate, adapt or republish substantial protected content, figures, tables, diagrams, toolkit material or guidance text unless the relevant licence or explicit permission allows it.

Public availability does not necessarily mean that material may be freely copied, translated, adapted or redistributed.

Where reuse conditions are unclear, the preferred approach is to summarise the relevant findings in original wording and provide a link to the original source.

Users should also avoid uploading or pasting substantial third-party copyrighted material into a public AI service unless authorised to do so under the applicable licence, permission, or organisational policy.

Use of external EBP resources

Resources such as the College of Policing Crime Reduction Toolkit, Campbell Collaboration, POP Center and Evidence-Based Policing Matrix are used primarily as evidence and navigation sources.

Where possible, the generated prompt directs the AI to trace findings back to the original study, review, official publication, publisher page or other formal source.

This website does not claim ownership of, or official affiliation with, these external resources.

Where translation, adaptation or republication rights are unclear, users should rely on summaries and links to the original source unless permission has been obtained.

Check the evidence

AI-generated summaries and recommendations may contain errors, omissions or incorrect interpretations.

Before using any evidence in operational planning, research, evaluation or decision-making:

  • check the original source;
  • confirm that the cited study or document exists;
  • verify important findings, statistics, URLs and DOIs;
  • distinguish established evidence from hypotheses or provisional interpretations; and
  • consider whether the evidence is transferable to the local context.

The practitioner and their organisation remain responsible for the final decision.

Before implementing an intervention or evaluation

A suggested evaluation design is not, by itself, approval to implement it.

Depending on the intervention and evaluation design, organisational approval, ethics review, privacy assessment, legal advice, information-governance review, consultation with a research partner, or other local approval may be required.

This is particularly important where an evaluation involves:

  • randomisation or differential treatment;
  • personal or identifiable data;
  • vulnerable populations;
  • sensitive police or administrative data;
  • surveillance;
  • experimental changes to operational practice; or
  • interventions that may affect rights, access, burden or risk.
Prototype status

This is an experimental prototype developed by Ichimizu-kai to explore how structured AI prompts might help practitioners engage with Evidence-Based Policing.

It is not an official police system, not a substitute for professional judgement, and not a substitute for organisational, legal, ethical, information-governance or research approval.

The tool will continue to be revised in response to practitioner, researcher and international feedback.

Read this before entering any information

Do not enter personal data, identifiable case details, active investigation information, sensitive operational methods, unpublished internal information, or confidential material.

Where necessary, anonymise or generalise details using labels such as “Area A”, “Facility A”, or “Target Group A”. This page does not send your entries to an external server; the prompt is generated locally in your browser.

STEP 1

Enter what you know

You do not need to complete every field. After you paste the prompt into your AI, it will ask for any additional information that would materially improve the analysis.

What problem are you trying to address?

Enter the relevant area, facility, population, or setting at an appropriate level of detail. Do not enter a precise street address.

Enter confirmed facts such as patterns, trends, observations, or available baseline information.

Leave blank if no intervention is currently in place.

Leave blank if you do not yet have an idea. The AI can compare several options.

A final outcome is enough. The AI can help identify relevant intermediate outcomes.

Enter data that already exist or that could realistically be collected.

These conditions help keep the intervention and evaluation realistic.

Include relevant partnerships, implementation restrictions, sequencing requirements, or other field conditions.

Before generating your prompt

  • Use only an AI service approved or permitted by your organisation.
  • Do not enter personal, investigative, operationally sensitive, confidential or non-public information.
  • Do not upload copyrighted third-party material unless authorised.
  • Check AI-generated evidence against the original sources.
  • Final decisions remain with the practitioner and their organisation.

STEP 2

Review your content and safety check

The browser runs a simple rule-based scan. It cannot detect every sensitive detail, so you must also review the content yourself.

Changes are required

The scan found content that looks like direct personal information. Remove or anonymise it before continuing.

Please check carefully

The scan found terms that may indicate operationally sensitive, investigative, non-public, or confidential information. Confirm that no such information is included before continuing.

What to do after copying the prompt

  1. Select “Copy the generated prompt”.
  2. Open an AI service that you are authorised by your organisation to use, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Copilot where permitted.
  3. Paste the prompt into a new chat and send it.
  4. Answer the AI’s follow-up questions and work through intervention options, measurement, and evaluation design.

This webpage does not itself send your information to an AI service. Use only AI services permitted by your organisation and follow all applicable information-security rules.
